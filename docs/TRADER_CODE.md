# The Trader's Code

Stavros, 2026-10-15: "I really like the below. it's more psychological but just so you can reference."

> Trading isn't about predicting every move. It's about waiting for your setup and managing your risk. The market doesn't care about what you think, what you need, or what you lost yesterday. Beginners chase entries. Consistent traders manage risk. Professionals know when to do nothing. A level touch isn't a trade, you wait for confirmation. Price action first, your opinion last. Let the market do the talking. Watch the order flow. Stop clicking, you're bored, not bullish. Trading is patience, then execution. Know your stop before your entry. If the risk doesn't fit, the trade doesn't fit. More contracts doesn't fix bad discipline, they make it more expensive. Add when the trade confirms, not when you're desperate for a bounce. Put a shot clock on your trade. If nothing is happening, get out. A winning trade can still be a bad decision. A losing trade can still be the right one. Take the loss, have a goldfish memory, move on to the next setup. After your stop gets hit, the trade is over. That's not negotiable. Protect your mental capital. No clear head, no trade. Missing a trade isn't losing money, chasing it can be. And most importantly, slow down.

## Where Moonbag already enforces it

| The code | How the desk holds you to it |
|---|---|
| Wait for your setup · a level touch isn't a trade | One setup: a real level, then a closed 1h candle (sweep-and-close-back or break-and-hold). Never the first touch or a wick. Limits sit *through* the level so they only fill on a sweep. |
| Know your stop before your entry | The 3-line plan opens every card: *Bias because · Entering when · Wrong if*. No "Wrong if", no trade. |
| If the risk doesn't fit, the trade doesn't fit | Size = risk $ ÷ stop distance. Aim for 2:1 with the real stop (no hard minimum since 2026-10-16 — anything under 2R is flagged "your call"); never pull the stop closer to make it fit. |
| More contracts doesn't fix bad discipline | Risk is a % by grade (2% max), halved after 2 losses in a row and on red-folder days. Leverage never changes the dollar risk. |
| Add when the trade confirms | Add only to a trade that has confirmed (in profit, stop at or above entry); never average down a loser. |
| Put a shot clock on your trade | 72h review line; open-drive and scalp tests exit by the session close; limits expire after 24–48h. |
| After your stop gets hit, the trade is over | The stop is on the exchange at entry. No re-entry on the same coin and side for the rest of that session without a fresh setup and a new 3-line plan. |
| Take the loss, goldfish memory | One loss-journal line (what happened, the check that would have caught it) — then it's closed. |
| Missing a trade isn't losing money | No chasing: a coin 10%+ off its 3-day low/high or > 2 daily ATRs from its 20 EMA is "cooling — wait". |
| Professionals know when to do nothing · slow down | NO TRADE is the default. Monday is plan day. Alerts only for action. |
| A winning trade can be a bad decision; a losing trade can be the right one | The Coach grades process (followed the plan?) separately from outcome (R). |
| No clear head, no trade | Grok asks one question before inputs after two losses in a day: "Clear head?" — a "no" ends it for the day. |
